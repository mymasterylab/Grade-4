The MyMasteryLab 3-Check System

Before serving any answer, run it through three checks:

1EstimateAbout how much should I have?
2InverseDoes the opposite operation confirm it?
3StrategyCan I prove it another way?
👨‍🍳 Chef's Motto: "I'm not finished when I get an answer. I'm finished when I can explain why it can be trusted."

A correct answer should hold up no matter which strategy you use:

Standard algorithm Decomposition Compensation Friendly numbers Number line Missing addend Mental calculation Estimation
Then ask: "Can I explain why my answer makes sense?"
🚨 Kitchen Case #1

"I subtracted each digit..."

70,000 g − 28,456 g = 51,544 g
1
Estimate: 70,000 − 28,000 ≈ 42,000. But 51,544 is nowhere close — suspicious!
2
Inverse: 28,456 + 51,544 = 80,000, not 70,000. The check fails.
3
Missing addend: 28,456 + ? = 70,000  ⇒  ? = 41,544
Correct answer: 70,000 − 28,456 = 41,544

The chef likely forgot to reduce a digit after exchanging it away during regrouping — a classic place-value slip, caught only by checking.

tap

Chef Challenge

72,000 g − 38,500 g

Three different strategies, one answer:

1
Standard algorithm: 72,000 − 38,500 = 33,500
2
Missing addend: 38,500 + ? = 72,000  ⇒  ? = 33,500
3
Counting up: from 38,500 to 72,000 is 33,500
All three strategies agree: 33,500 g. Different correct strategies must always land on the same answer.

🍕 The Pizza Kitchen

A chef has 24,500 g of flour, receives 13,750 g more, then uses 8,500 g to make pizzas. How much flour is left?

1
Add the new flour: 24,500 + 13,750 = 38,250 g
2
Subtract what was used: 38,250 − 8,500 = 29,750 g
✅ Answer: 29,750 g of flour left

Estimate: 25,000 + 14,000 = 39,000, then 39,000 − 9,000 ≈ 30,000 — very close! Check: 29,750 + 8,500 = 38,250, then 38,250 − 13,750 = 24,500 — back to the start ✓
