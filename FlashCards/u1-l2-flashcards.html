Clue #1

Spot the pattern

Each number below has a 4 in a different house. Starting from the right, watch what happens to its value each time it moves one house to the left.

40,000 ← 4,000 ← 400 ← 40 ← 4

Every time the 4 moves one house to the left, its value becomes 10 times bigger.

Going backwards works too — division undoes multiplication.

Undo it with ÷10
40 ÷ 10= 4
400 ÷ 10= 40
4,000 ÷ 10= 400
Clue #2 · The Why

It's not about zeros

We do not say "add a zero." We ask: what happens to the value?

Drag rule: one house left = ×10  |  one house right = ÷10

Move RIGHT ➡
The digit 4 currently sits in the Hundreds house → value 400.
Clue #3 · Don't Be Fooled

The digits didn't vanish

Compare these two ways of writing the same idea:

5,000 ÷ 10= 500
5,000 → 500
🕵️ Case notes: The 5 didn't disappear or get replaced. It moved from the Thousands house to the Hundreds house — its place value changed, not its digit.
Some learners think zeros are "removed" randomly. Really, every digit shifts one house to the right when we divide by 10 — the 5 moves from Thousands to Hundreds, and the trailing zeros shift too.

Suspect Statement

Fix the detective's error

A learner wrote:

"4,500 × 10 = 4,5000"
Just tacking on a zero at the end gives a number written incorrectly. Every digit needs to shift one house left: 4,500 × 10 = 45,000.

Bonus Clue

Find the missing number

× 10 × 10 = 7,000
Two ways to solve it:
1️⃣ Work backwards: 7,000 ÷ 10 = 700, then 700 ÷ 10 = 70.
2️⃣ Combine the moves: ×10 ×10 is the same as ×100, so 7,000 ÷ 100 = 70.

Explain in your own words

Why is 70 ten times 7?

Model answer: 70 sits one house to the left of 7 on the place-value street (Tens instead of Ones), so its value is 7 × 10 = 70.
