The Secret Rule

1 larger unit = 10 smaller units

If a place has zero, we don't stop — we exchange from the next larger place:

1 thousand
↓ exchange for ↓
10 hundreds
↓ exchange for ↓
10 tens
↓ exchange for ↓
10 ones

Every exchange keeps the exact same value — only the place-value form changes.

Clue #2 · The Why

Zeros don't block subtraction

Instead of "borrowing," use the correct language:

✗ "BORROW FROM THE NEXT COLUMN"
✓ "EXCHANGE 1 LARGER UNIT FOR 10 SMALLER UNITS"

No! Zero just means no units are available yet in that place — the place still exists. We simply continue the exchange through the next larger place until we reach the place we need.

Consecutive Zeros

20,000 − 7,845

Four zeros in a row — watch the exchange cascade all the way down:

1
2 ten-thousands → 1 stays, 1 exchanges for 10 thousands
2
1 of those thousands → 10 hundreds
3
1 of those hundreds → 10 tens
4
1 of those tens → 10 ones
20,000 − 7,845 = 12,155

Every zero became a 9 (after lending 1 away) except the very last place, which received the full 10. The value never changed — only its shape.
